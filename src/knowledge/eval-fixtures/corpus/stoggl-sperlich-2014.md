# Polarized training compared with other intensity distributions

## Study design

Forty-eight well-trained runners, cyclists, triathletes and cross-country skiers were assigned to one of four nine-week training programs: high-volume low-intensity training, threshold training, high-intensity interval training, or polarized training. The polarized program combined a large amount of low-intensity work with a smaller block of very hard intervals and almost no threshold work. Peak oxygen uptake, time to exhaustion in a ramp test, peak velocity or power, and lactate threshold were measured before and after.

## Results

The polarized group showed the largest improvements: peak oxygen uptake rose by about 11.7%, time to exhaustion by about 17%, and peak velocity or power by about 5%. The interval group also improved several markers. The threshold group and the high-volume group improved little, and some markers of the threshold group got worse.

## Interpretation

For athletes who already train a lot, a polarized distribution produced greater gains than simply adding volume or spending more time at threshold. The authors suggest that at least one short phase of polarized training is worth trying when endurance performance plateaus.
