# Heart rate variability monitoring in endurance athletes

## What to measure

The natural logarithm of the root mean square of successive differences (lnRMSSD) is a practical vagal index. It should be measured every morning after waking, in the same posture, before coffee or training. A short one-minute recording after a short stabilisation period is enough for daily use.

## Weekly averages over single days

Single-day values are noisy and can change with sleep, alcohol, stress or hydration. A rolling seven-day average of lnRMSSD, together with its coefficient of variation, gives a much clearer picture of how the athlete is adapting. Decisions should not be based on one low morning value.

## Interpreting trends

A falling weekly average, especially with a rising coefficient of variation, can indicate accumulated fatigue or non-functional overreaching. A stable or rising average during a training block suggests positive adaptation. When the trend points down for several days alongside poor sleep or high perceived fatigue, reducing intensity for a few days is sensible.
