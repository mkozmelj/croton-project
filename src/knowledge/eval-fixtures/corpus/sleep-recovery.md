# Sleep and recovery

## How much sleep

Athletes sleeping less than seven hours a night show slower reaction times, higher perceived effort and a higher injury rate. Extending sleep to nine or ten hours for several weeks improved sprint times and accuracy in team-sport athletes and improved mood.

## Improving sleep

A regular bedtime, a cool dark room and limiting screens and caffeine late in the day help. Short naps of 20-30 minutes in the early afternoon can make up for a short night. Alcohol shortens deep sleep and lowers overnight heart rate variability.

## Using sleep data

Wearable sleep scores are useful for trends over weeks, not for judging single nights. Several short nights in a row are a good reason to make the next hard session easier.
