# The training-injury prevention paradox

## High chronic load protects

Athletes who have built up a high chronic training load over several weeks are more resilient to injury than athletes with low chronic load. Well-developed fitness allows them to tolerate hard sessions and competitions. Training hard is therefore not the problem in itself; the problem is getting there too quickly.

## Acute:chronic workload ratio

The acute:chronic workload ratio compares the load of the last week with the average weekly load of the last four weeks. A ratio between about 0.8 and 1.3 was described as a sweet spot with the lowest injury likelihood. Ratios above 1.5 were described as a danger zone, where injury likelihood rose sharply in the following week.

## Load progression

Week-to-week increases in load of more than about 10-15% were associated with more injuries. Coaches are advised to build chronic load progressively, avoid spikes after holidays, illness or taper weeks, and monitor both absolute load and the ratio.
