# Criticism of the acute:chronic workload ratio

## Statistical problems

Because the acute week is also part of the chronic average, the two values are mathematically coupled, and the ratio can show associations that come from this coupling rather than from physiology. Ratios also behave poorly when the chronic load is small, and many different load patterns produce the same ratio.

## Evidence for injury prediction

Re-analysis of the published data found no good evidence that the ratio predicts injury for an individual athlete, or that managing training by the ratio prevents injuries. Associations seen at group level do not translate into a useful individual prediction tool.

## Recommendation

The authors recommend dismissing the ratio as a framework for injury risk. Load should still be monitored and progressed gradually, but decisions should rest on the athlete's history, symptoms and the plan's logic rather than on one number crossing a threshold.
