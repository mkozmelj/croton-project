# Race fueling for long endurance events

## Carbohydrate intake

For events longer than about two and a half hours, taking in 60-90 g of carbohydrate per hour supports performance. Intakes above 60 g per hour require mixtures of glucose and fructose, because they use different gut transporters; ratios of 2:1 or 1:0.8 are common. Shorter events of about an hour need little more than a mouth rinse.

## Training the gut

The gut adapts to regular carbohydrate intake during exercise. Practise race-day fueling in long training sessions for several weeks so that high intakes are tolerated without cramps or nausea.

## Fluids and sodium

Drinking to thirst works for most athletes in moderate conditions; heavy sweaters in the heat may need a plan based on measured sweat rate. Sodium losses vary widely, and around 300-600 mg of sodium per hour is a reasonable starting point for long events, with more for salty sweaters.
